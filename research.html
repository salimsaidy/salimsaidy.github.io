Research

I study how digital finance, financial institutions, and public policy shape economic opportunity and resilience. My research combines applied econometrics, causal machine learning, and computational text analysis, with a focus on Sub-Saharan Africa and other developing economies.

Journal article · 2026

Islamic finance development and FinTech innovation: pathways to financial inclusion in OIC and non-OIC countries ↗

Bekiroğlu, A. M., Saidy, S., Vergil, H., & Hassan, A. D. (2026). International Journal of Islamic and Middle Eastern Finance and Management, 1–26.

Using evidence from 64 countries, this study examines how Islamic finance development and digital innovation relate to financial inclusion. It finds that institutional development supports Islamic FinTech innovation, while wider access to financial services also depends on translating that capacity into usable, accessible products.

Citation

Bekiroğlu, A. M., Saidy, S., Vergil, H., & Hassan, A. D. (2026). Islamic finance development and FinTech innovation: pathways to financial inclusion in OIC and non-OIC countries. International Journal of Islamic and Middle Eastern Finance and Management, 1–26. https://doi.org/10.1108/IMEFM-04-2026-0293

Journal article · 2026

Institutional quality, FDI effectiveness, and economic growth in the Gambia ↗

Saidy, S., & Hassan, A. D. (2026). SN Business & Economics, 6, Article 241.

This study examines foreign direct investment, institutional quality, and economic growth in The Gambia using annual data from 1996 to 2023. Both investment and institutional quality are positively associated with growth, but the analysis finds no statistically significant evidence that institutional quality strengthens the effect of foreign investment.

Citation

Saidy, S., & Hassan, A. D. (2026). Institutional quality, FDI effectiveness, and economic growth in the Gambia. SN Business & Economics, 6, Article 241. https://doi.org/10.1007/s43546-026-01256-9

Journal article · 2026

The conflict-growth nexus in Fragile States: Empirical evidence from Somalia ↗

Hassan, A. D., & Saidy, S. (2026). International Journal of Social Sciences Perspectives, 17(1), 25–35.

Using annual data from 1997 to 2022, this study examines the long-run relationship between armed conflict and economic growth in Somalia. Conflict and unemployment are linked to weaker economic performance, while foreign aid is positively associated with growth, highlighting the importance of peace and institutional rebuilding for recovery.

Citation

Hassan, A. D., & Saidy, S. (2026). The conflict-growth nexus in Fragile States: Empirical evidence from Somalia. International Journal of Social Sciences Perspectives, 17(1), 25–35. https://doi.org/10.33094/ijssp.v17i1.2436

Journal article · 2025

Modeling drivers of fintech adoption in Sub-Saharan Africa using machine learning ↗

Saidy, S., & Hassan, A. D. (2025). Digital Finance, 7, 1065–1091.

Drawing on the 2021 Global Findex survey across 36 Sub-Saharan African countries, this study combines econometric and machine-learning models to understand FinTech adoption. Formal account ownership, mobile phones, and internet connectivity emerge as strong predictors, with important differences across income groups, education levels, and locations.

Citation

Saidy, S., & Hassan, A. D. (2025). Modeling drivers of fintech adoption in Sub-Saharan Africa using machine learning. Digital Finance, 7, 1065–1091. https://doi.org/10.1007/s42521-025-00161-4

Journal article · 2025

Examining foreign capital inflows and growth in The Gambia: A dual-gap approach ↗

Vergil, H., & Saidy, S. (2025). South African Journal of Economic and Management Sciences, 28(1), Article a6031.

This study examines how foreign capital relates to economic growth in The Gambia using annual data from 1980 to 2023. Foreign direct investment and remittances are positively associated with growth, while foreign exchange constraints emerge as a significant obstacle to development.

Citation

Vergil, H., & Saidy, S. (2025). Examining foreign capital inflows and growth in The Gambia: A dual-gap approach. South African Journal of Economic and Management Sciences, 28(1), Article a6031. https://doi.org/10.4102/sajems.v28i1.6031

Current research questions

My ongoing research explores the following questions:

  • How do people experience AI assistants, and what shapes satisfaction, trust and continued use?
  • How does digital finance affect households’ ability to cope with financial shocks, and who benefits most?
  • How do the depth of financial inclusion and the quality of digital connectivity shape the use of digital financial services?
  • When does digital access expand women’s economic opportunities, and what barriers limit its benefits?
  • How do gender, location and household circumstances shape access to formal employment?
  • Do skills and job availability predict which workers change occupations, and where they go?
  • How are AI and disruptive technologies reshaping labour-market inclusion?
